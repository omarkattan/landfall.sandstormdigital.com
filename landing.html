How it works

Connect once. Then it watches.

Landfall reads Search Console directly. There is nothing to install on your site, no tag, no script, and no change to how you work.

01 · CONNECT

Add a reader to Search Console

You grant read-only access to one email address, the same way you would add a colleague. Landfall pulls sixteen months of history on the first run, which is everything Google keeps.

02 · WATCH

Updates get matched to your data

Every ranking update Google announces is picked up automatically. Your traffic is split into pages, query intent, and branded against non-branded, then measured against what each part should have done.

03 · ACT

Read the verdict, do the work

Which segments were hit, whether it looks algorithmic or self-inflicted, and specific work ranked by the clicks at stake. Named pages and queries, not general advice.

Why it is different

Most tools tell you an update happened. You already knew.

It knows what should have happened

Traffic falls at weekends and rises in season. Landfall compares each day against the same weekday over the previous eight weeks, so a normal Sunday never reads as a catastrophe. When an update lands, that expectation is frozen the day before the rollout and projected forward, which is the only way to measure damage that the passage of time has already normalised.

It can tell algorithmic from self-inflicted

If parts of your site fell while others held, that is Google reassessing you. If everything fell together, an update is the wrong explanation and something on your side broke. Landfall makes that call explicitly, which saves you the week normally spent blaming the wrong thing.

It sees clicks taken above the results

When impressions and position hold but clicks fall, your listing is still there and people are not choosing it. That is the signature of an AI Overview or a snippet sitting above you, and it calls for entirely different work than a ranking loss. A tool that only watches clicks cannot see the difference.

It works on the quiet days too

Updates are rare. Work is not. Between them, Landfall surfaces queries within reach of the top, pages ranking well that nobody clicks, and content decaying so slowly that no alert would ever fire, each with the clicks at stake attached.

Questions

Straight answers

What access do you need to my site?

Read-only access to your Search Console property, granted the same way you would add a colleague. You add one email address under Users and permissions, at the Restricted level, which cannot change any setting.

Nothing is installed on your site. No tag, no script, no DNS change, no code review. Revoking access is one click and takes effect immediately.

How long before it tells me anything useful?

The first pull brings in sixteen months of history, which is Google's full retention window, so past updates are assessed immediately. That usually takes a few hours.

For a new update, Landfall waits until the rollout finishes and at least fourteen days have passed before judging it. Reading a rollout while it is still moving produces confident nonsense, and Google says as much themselves.

What if nothing happened to my site?

Then it says so. Landfall will report that an update did not move your site in a way the data can distinguish from normal variation, and give you no recommendations for it.

That is a real finding, not a failure. It rules the update out as an explanation, which means whatever you are seeing has another cause and you can stop looking there.

Does it use AI to write the recommendations?

No. Every statement is produced from the numbers by fixed rules, so the same data always produces the same words and every sentence traces back to a measurement.

Generated prose would be easier to build and impossible to trust. When a tool tells a client their blog lost a third of its traffic, that claim has to be defensible.

Which search engines does it cover?

Google today, using Search Console as the source. Bing is next, and it unlocks the strongest diagnostic there is: a drop in Google that Bing did not share is almost certainly algorithmic, while a drop in both usually means something on your side broke.

AI assistant visibility and international engines follow after that.

Who is it for?

Agencies and in-house teams who have to explain a traffic drop to somebody who is not an SEO. The output is written to be forwarded, not translated.

If you manage several sites, the same picture works across a portfolio: which clients an update touched, and which it left alone.

What happens to my data?

Your Search Console figures are stored so history can be compared over time, and they are used for your reports only. Nothing is sold, shared, or pooled with other accounts.

Remove the access grant and the data stops updating. Ask for deletion and it goes.
